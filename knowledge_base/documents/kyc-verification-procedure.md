# KYC Verification Procedure

> SYNTHETIC POLICY. Invented for the FIN-GUARD research prototype. It is not real regulation, legal advice or any bank's actual procedure.

## Purpose and scope
This procedure describes how an analyst verifies that a customer's identity documents agree with the customer master record. It applies to every customer whose KYC status is pending, expired or flagged by the automated entity matcher. The automated matcher only proposes candidates and evidence; a human analyst makes the final decision.

## Verifying identity documents
Compare the name, date of birth and address on the identity document with the customer record. Confirm the document type is accepted, the document number is present and the document has not expired. Record every attribute that agrees and every attribute that conflicts. A single agreeing attribute, such as a matching name, is never sufficient on its own to confirm identity.

## Name variations
Legitimate customers often appear under different spellings. Common causes are typing errors, initials instead of full first names, a missing or different middle name, transliteration between alphabets (for example Mohammed, Muhammad and Mohamed) and surname-first ordering. These variations alone are not grounds for rejection. Treat a name variation as acceptable only when the date of birth and address also agree, or when a second document explains the difference.

## Date-of-birth conflicts
A date of birth that differs between the document and the record is a serious contradiction. Check first for a transposed day and month or a one-digit typing error. If the conflict is not clearly a data-entry slip, request a second identity document and set the case to REVIEW. Never overwrite the stored date of birth without a second document and analyst sign-off.

## Address mismatches
An address mismatch is weaker evidence than a date-of-birth conflict because customers move house. A changed house number, street or city is acceptable when the name and date of birth agree and the customer can supply recent proof of address. A different country of residence needs additional review.

## Duplicate customer records
Two customer records that share a date of birth and a similar name may be the same person registered twice. Do not merge records automatically. An analyst must review both records, confirm the match with a second attribute such as address or document number, and obtain sign-off before any merge.

## Namesakes
Different people can share the same name. When two records have identical names but different dates of birth and different addresses, treat them as different people. Never link records on name similarity alone.
