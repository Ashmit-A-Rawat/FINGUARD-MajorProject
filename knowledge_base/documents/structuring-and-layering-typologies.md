# Structuring and Layering Typologies

> SYNTHETIC POLICY. Invented for the FIN-GUARD research prototype. It is not real regulation or legal advice. These typologies are simplified illustrations.

## Structuring
Structuring is splitting a large sum into several smaller transfers so that no single transfer looks unusual or crosses a reporting limit. Indicators are several transfers of similar or identical size in a short period to the same recipient, amounts just below a round threshold, and activity that stops once the total is reached. A repeated-transfer pattern to a new beneficiary is the most common indicator.

## Rapid movement of funds
Funds that arrive and are moved out again within minutes or hours, with little of the balance retained, may indicate that the account is being used only as a pass-through. Look for an incoming transfer followed closely by outgoing transfers of a similar total, especially to new counterparties or other countries.

## Round-amount patterns
Payments in exact round amounts, such as 1,000 or 5,000, are common in legitimate rent and instalments but also appear in suspicious activity. A round amount alone is not a signal; it matters when it is combined with a new beneficiary, an unusual country or a burst of similar payments.
