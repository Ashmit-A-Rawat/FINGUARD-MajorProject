# Transaction Monitoring Policy

> SYNTHETIC POLICY. Invented for the FIN-GUARD research prototype. It is not real regulation or legal advice. Numeric thresholds are illustrative.

## Purpose and definitions
Transaction monitoring looks for behaviour that departs from a customer's own history or from normal population behaviour. An anomaly is a reason to look closer, not proof of wrongdoing. Every anomaly finding must be supported by concrete transaction evidence and reviewed by a person before any action.

## High-value transactions
A transaction whose amount is many times larger than the customer's typical amount, for example more than five times, is a high-value anomaly. Compare the amount with the customer's own history, not with a global limit, because a business account legitimately moves larger sums than a student account. Consider whether a known event, such as a property purchase, explains the amount.

## Rapid bursts
A burst is five or more transactions from one customer within ten minutes, especially to the same new beneficiary. Bursts can indicate automated activity, account takeover or an attempt to stay below per-transaction limits. Check whether the channel and device were the same throughout.

## Abnormal velocity
Abnormal velocity means an unusually large number of transactions in a day, for example fifteen or more within twenty-four hours for a customer who normally makes a few per week. Unlike a burst, the activity is spread across the day. Compare the customer's daily transaction counts over the previous weeks.

## Unusual transaction time
A transaction between one and five in the morning, when the customer normally transacts during the day, is an unusual-time anomaly. Time alone is a weak signal. It becomes stronger when combined with a high amount, a new beneficiary or a change of channel.

## Geographic change
A payment sent to or received from a country the customer has never dealt with before is a geographic change. It is more significant when the customer is normally domestic and the country is unusual for the customer's profile. Travel and relocation are common legitimate explanations and should be checked.

## New counterparties
A first-time beneficiary is normal for many customers, so a new counterparty is only worth attention when combined with another signal, most often an amount well above the customer's norm. Record whether the customer has any prior relationship with the counterparty.

## Repeated identical transfers
Several transfers of exactly the same amount to the same new beneficiary within a few weeks, typically four or more, form a repeated-transfer pattern. Legitimate causes include rent, instalments and salaries; check whether the customer has a recurring arrangement.
